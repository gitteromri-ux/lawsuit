# Claude (Anthropic): what the terms promise, what the refund policy allows, where a claim can stand

Prepared 10 November 2026. Sources at the end. Not legal advice; Claude is not a lawyer.

## 1. The refund policy, exactly

- Consumer Terms: except as expressly provided in the Terms or where required by law, all payments are non-refundable.
- The Terms carry only narrow refund promises: a pro-rata refund if Anthropic ends a subscription for a reason other than a terms violation; no refund if access is ended for a violation.
- Help Center route for web purchases: initials > Get help > Send us a message > "Claude Refund Request" > pick a reason > automated eligibility check. A refund request does not cancel the plan.
- App Store purchases are refunded only by Apple (reportaproblem.apple.com). Google Play purchases need an eligibility check by Anthropic Support.
- EEA and UK consumers: refund within 14 days of purchase, prorated by use. Not stated as worldwide.
- Upgrading Pro or Max to Team: automatic prorated refund.
- An open bank dispute or chargeback blocks Anthropic from processing a refund while it is pending.

## 2. What the Terms say the product does (the "legitimate behavior" baseline)

The Consumer Terms (effective 8 Oct 2025, current version) say, in substance:
- Outputs may be inaccurate and may contain material inaccuracies even when they look detailed and specific.
- Actions (software manipulation, data processing, system interactions done on your behalf) may not be error free or operate as you intended.
- You should not rely on Outputs or Actions without independently confirming their accuracy.
- Warranties are disclaimed and consequential damages waived, except non-waivable consumer rights.
- Usage is subject to plan limits ("Technical Limitations") described on the purchase pages.

**Hard truth for the case:** a wrong output, a broken file, or a failed action is, on paper, inside the behavior the Terms describe. A claim built on "the work was bad" alone will be answered with this clause.

## 3. Where a claim is stronger: conduct the disclaimer does not cleanly cover

The disclaimer warns that outputs can be wrong. It does not say the agent will state it verified or audited something it did not check. The strongest cases are therefore these, in order:

1. **False verification statements.** Claude says "verified", "audited", "checked on device", "live", and the same transcript shows it was not. This is a representation about the service's own conduct, not an inaccurate output about the world.
2. **Contradictory capability claims.** In one session Claude says it cannot edit or render video in any format; in others it renders and delivers MP4 files. The customer is told different things about what the paid product does.
3. **Compute spent after the agent knew, or should have checked, that the result could not be delivered.** Example: third-party generation credits spent before confirming the files could be downloaded from the sandbox.
4. **Repetition after explicit correction.** The same defect delivered again after the customer named it and the agent acknowledged it. Shows the failure is systemic, not a one-off inaccuracy.
5. **Usage-limit marketing.** A class action filed 8 September 2026 alleges Max plans were sold as 5x or 20x more usage without clear disclosure of weekly caps; an individual federal suit on similar grounds was filed in June 2026. Relevant only if your spend is on Max.

## 4. What forums actually show about refunds

- No evidence found of Anthropic paying large refunds for output quality. Public reports run the other way: refunds refused even where support acknowledged a billing bug; one Max user waited over a month on erroneous "Extra Usage" charges, routed to an AI support agent and an in-app flow that does not cover extra usage.
- Refunds that do get granted are routine: billing errors, early annual cancellations switched to monthly, Team upgrades, EEA/UK 14-day window.
- Credits in third-party tools (Artlist, Higgsfield and similar) are billed by those companies, not Anthropic, and are outside Anthropic's refund policy entirely.

## 5. Weak points you should fix before sending anything

- **Business use.** You use Claude for an agency. The EEA terms define a consumer as someone acting wholly or mainly outside their trade. Business use weakens consumer-protection arguments; Israeli consumer law may treat this similarly. Get an Israeli lawyer's read.
- **The dollar figure.** The claim needs your actual invoices: Anthropic plan and extra-usage charges, separate from third-party tool credits. "Hundreds of thousands" will be discounted unless each dollar is on an invoice.
- **Verbatim evidence.** Each case needs Claude's exact words and the contradiction in the same chat. Summaries do not count. The counter enforces this: "proven" requires both quotes.
- **Do not open a chargeback while asking support for a refund**; the dispute blocks their refund process.

## 6. How to send it

1. Run the in-app "Claude Refund Request" first so there is a dated, denied or granted request on record.
2. Email support with the counter link, the 5 strongest proven cases, invoice totals, and the specific remedy asked for.
3. Keep every reply; log each response date in the counter.
